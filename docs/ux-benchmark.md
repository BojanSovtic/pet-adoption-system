# UX benchmark and redesign roadmap

_Researched 2026-10-26. This doc covers what we learned from established adoption sites, how our app compares, and the order in which we'll improve it._

## 2. Patterns compared

| Area                                        | What we observed                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                | Worth adopting here?                                                                                                                                    |
| ------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Navigation and information architecture** | Every site separates "find a pet" from "how adoption works". RSPCA has a "Jump to: process · what we need from you · fees · FAQs" bar. Breadcrumbs on detail pages (Battersea, Dogs Trust).                                                                                                                                                     | **Yes:** a back link to the list on details, and adoption steps explained in context. We have one flow, so no mega-menus are needed.                    |
| **Search and filtering**                    | Battersea filters by **lifestyle fit** (lives with children/cats/dogs, time left alone, the adopter's own age) as well as attributes. Dogs Trust puts filters behind a "0 filters added +" drawer, with sort next to it. Best Friends uses many dropdowns plus a name search. Petfinder (secondary) offers saved-search email alerts.           | Our search + species + status + sort row already covers our data. Lifestyle filters are the strongest idea, but they need new pet fields (see Batch 5). |
| **Pet cards and density**                   | 3–4 columns on desktop and 1 on mobile everywhere. Cards show photo, name, 2–3 facts (breed, location) and **fact chips** (sex · age · size). Status shown on the photo ("Reserved" ribbon, "Underdog" tag). Dogs Trust puts a heart (favourite) on every card.                                                                                 | We already have photo + overlay status + name + facts. Chips only pay off once we have more attributes.                                                 |
| **Detail page and photos**                  | A large gallery first, then a **"1 / 16" counter** and thumbnails (Battersea). The **apply button sits right beside the name/summary** (Dogs Trust: "Apply to adopt" + "Add to favourites"). Key facts in a **scannable table or tiles** before the long story (Battersea, Dogs Trust). Separate sections for "living with children/dogs/cats". | **Yes, high value:** photo first with a sensible crop, facts as tiles, and the apply action near the top (beside the photo on desktop).                 |
| **Calls to action**                         | One dominant action per page ("Apply to adopt"). Battersea is the counter-example: no apply button, and the route to apply is an email address and a link buried at the end of a long text.                                                                                                                                                     | **Yes:** a single clear primary action. A signed-out visitor should get a real "Sign in to apply" button, not small text.                               |
| **Forms and validation**                    | Not observable (behind sign-in). RSPCA sets expectations before the form: what's needed, then "you should hear back within 48 hours", then a numbered process.                                                                                                                                                                                  | **Yes:** explain what to write and what happens next beside our application form.                                                                       |
| **Empty, loading and error states**         | Mostly invisible in normal use. Dogs Trust shows a silhouette placeholder for a dog without photos.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             | We already have skeletons and states on `/pets`; extend them to the details page.                                                                       |
| **Mobile**                                  | Everything stacks and the gallery shrinks. **Negative:** Dogs Trust and Best Friends spend the whole first mobile screen on a hero or promo, so the first pet appears only after scrolling. Dogs Trust's CTA panel stacks right under the name, which works well.                                                                               | Keep results above the fold (we do). On details, put facts and the apply panel straight after the photo on mobile.                                      |
| **Accessibility**                           | RSPCA has a "Colour mode" switch. Battersea uses visible checkbox labels. Pages relying on small, low-contrast text for key info (status ribbons in small caps) are harder to read.                                                                                                                                                             | Keep visible text labels, AA contrast and keyboard focus (already in the design system).                                                                |

**Takeaways:** the polished, illustration-heavy sites aren't necessarily the easiest to use. Battersea looks plain, but its filters and fact table are the most useful. Dogs Trust's playful hero delays the content on mobile. We take the _principles_: photo first, scannable facts, one clear action, expectations set before committing, and confirmation for irreversible steps. No layouts, wording or assets are copied.

Batch 5 is worth the backend effort only if the project should demonstrate filtering on richer data. It is the one research finding that changes the _matching_ quality, not just the presentation. Batch 6 is a common pattern, but it's lower value for a single-shelter-sized dataset.

## 5. UX decisions taken (Batch 1)

- **Confirm final decisions inline**, like delete: Accept and Reject switch to a short warning plus Confirm/Cancel. Withdraw doesn't ask, because the applicant can apply again afterwards.
- **Use the 4:3 photo ratio throughout**, the same as the cards, so the photo people clicked looks the same on the details page.
- **On desktop the adoption panel sits beside the gallery and stays visible while scrolling.** On mobile it comes straight after the photo, before the long description.
- **Set expectations next to the form**: three steps that match the real business rules (apply → owner reviews → accepted means adopted; withdraw while pending).
